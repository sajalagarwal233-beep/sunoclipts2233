# SaaS Activation & Onboarding

## When to Use

- The user asks about activating a newly signed-up SaaS user, or about activation as a metric or stage.
- The user asks about onboarding, onboarding flows, new-user tours, onboarding emails, setup checklists, training webinars, welcome sequences or first-run experience.
- The user asks about trial-to-paid conversion, reducing drop-off between signup and first value, or the "Aha" moment.
- The user asks about personal outreach to users, watching/observing user behaviour in the product, or asking users why they did not activate.
- The user asks about friction in signup or setup, or about conversion rate optimisation in the activation step.

## When NOT to Use

- The user wants a complete, ready-to-run onboarding playbook. The source does not contain one; only an outline exists.
- The user asks about pricing, growth channels, churn, retention after activation, or other parts of the book outside this chapter.
- The user needs the author's specific advice on any listed topic. On almost every topic the source has no body text, so do not present the outline entries as the book's advice.

## What The Book Actually Says

Everything below is the full content of `book/activiation/activation.md`, restated. It is an outline; verbatim wording is kept where it matters.

- Section heading "What is Activation?" with:
  - Activation is characterised as "Selling, educating, and usability (mostly usability)".
  - "What's my motivation?" — identity what motivates users and what keeps them from activating; the author points back to the basics of user profiles: you need to know who you are selling to.
  - "Friction" — friction is not your friend; why are you keeping your friends from that "'Aha' moment"; how to reduce friction.
- Section heading "Activation & Iteration" with:
  - CRO (conversion rate optimisation).
  - How people interact with your SaaS.
  - Figuring out how people interact with you, via "Stalking your users" (sub-items: "Every Step You Take…" listing LuckyOrange and HotJar; and the aside "It's like food porn, without the food"), via "Asking" (sub-items: the many ways of asking the same question — emails, texts, messages, chatbot; and "Publicly shaming yourself - the story of asking people to test broken software"), and via "Getting personal" (sub-items: stopping in, saying hello, asking for help, more stalking) and "Getting very personal - Dating your customers" (with the aside "Don't knock it till you try it!").
- Section heading "Onboarding" with:
  - Make it easy and cut to the chase.
  - Change is inevitable (you're going to iterate).
  - Value now, not later.
  - Value proposition as you progress.
  - "Onboarding made easy-er": login with social; quick login; pre-filled forms (checks on forms make it easier); simple language; help people understand how to use your system (list tutorial systems); help people import important information as easily as possible; "You're almost there" instead of "check your email"; a progress bar for getting set up.
  - "System education & outreach": the CEO email; focus (the cursor); learn by doing; make 'em dig deep to get confused (hide advanced features); onboarding emails x 5 or more.
- Section heading "Simple things to do on the website to assist in activation" with:
  - Say "It's Free".
  - Social proof.
  - Localization.
  - Don't be a CTA wimp - give me a reason to click your button.
  - "Submitting is something you do to someone in wrestling. Personally, I never submit."

No other claims, figures or recommendations exist in the source files.
